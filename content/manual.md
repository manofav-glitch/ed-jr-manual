## 1. General instructions for patient assessment & triage in ED

This manual aims to orient the junior residents (JRs) working in the Department of Emergency Medicine, AIIMS, New Delhi, about the common emergencies encountered during their shift. The goal is to give evidence-based standard of care to patients in a high-volume and high-acuity setup. Although every attempt is made to make it as current and complete as possible, situations will undoubtedly arise which are not included in the manual. **All residents are requested to read this manual before starting their residency.**

### General instructions

1. The Department of Emergency Medicine is divided into four zones:
   - Screening Emergency (initial assessment and triage)
   - Main Emergency (surgical patients)
   - New Emergency (medical patients)
   - Paediatric Emergency
2. Senior Residents (SRs) are the team leaders on the shift and are responsible for allotting the JRs in different areas.
3. The New Emergency is further divided into Red areas, Yellow areas and counter.
4. Red areas have critically ill patients and are usually manned by the Academic Junior Residents.
5. The 1st assessment after triage is done by the counter JR, who — after history, examination and relevant investigations — shifts the patient to appropriate areas in consultation with the SR.
6. Red patients are priority; if a resident is evaluating a yellow/green patient and is allotted a red patient, he/she has to attend the red patient first.
7. In the yellow areas, the junior resident has to write progress notes with vitals and ensure further work-up and consultations as per SR's instruction. A written handover has to be given to the next team in the given format.
8. Whenever **CODE BLUE** is announced, all JRs who are not attending critical patients should report to the concerned area.
9. Residents should report on time for their shifts. Remember the dictum: *"If you are five minutes early you are on time, if you are on time you are late."*
10. Mature interactions are expected with patients as well as with office and hospital personnel at all times.
11. Ensure clear & legible documentation in the case sheet of the patient. Document your name, time and date while writing the notes.
12. **Start antibiotics only after consultation with your Senior Resident.**

### Triage

Triage is the process of determining the priority of patients' treatments by the severity of their condition or likelihood of recovery with and without treatment.

In the Emergency Department, sequence of treatment of patients is determined by the patient's **triage category** and not on a first-come-first-serve basis.

In case of large numbers of patients and longer waiting times, **periodic reassessment & re-triaging** of patients is important.

**Triage categories: RED, YELLOW & GREEN**

#### RED — First priority (require immediate assessment & care)

**Compromised primary survey**

| | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **A — Airway** | Noisy breathing / stridor / pooling of secretions; angioedema involving face |
| **B — Breathing** | RR < 10 or > 24/min; SpO₂ < 94%; increased work of breathing, audible wheeze |
| **C — Circulation** | HR < 50 or > 120 (without fever); SBP < 90 or > 200; DBP > 110; shock index (pulse rate / SBP) > 1; presence of active bleeding |
| **D — Disability** | GCS < 13; responding only to pain or unresponsive; patient with ongoing seizure |

**Time-sensitive emergencies**

- Acute chest pain < 24 hr duration (? ACS)
- Suspected stroke < 24 hr (in window period)
- Drowning / hanging / electrocution / trauma with dangerous mechanism of injury
- Acute limb ischemia < 48 hr duration
- Acute scrotal / inguinal pain in young male
- Sudden onset abdominal pain, or pain with vaginal bleeding
- Toxin ingestion / bites & stings
- Any evaluation suggestive of sepsis
- Pregnancy in third trimester with pain abdomen or bleeding per vagina
- Severe pain anywhere in body (pain score > 7)
- H/o syncope
- Sudden onset severe headache (? SAH)
- Agitated / violent patient
- Acute urinary retention
- Fever, temperature > 39 °C with any one of: aplastic anaemia, acute leukaemia, h/o chemotherapy in last 14 days
- Outside reports of S. potassium > 5.5 mEq/L

**Priority Red even without above criteria:** AIIMS EHS patient

#### YELLOW — Second priority

Require emergent care, however can withstand some delay in comparison to Red-triaged patients.

**Stable primary survey**

- Patent airway
- RR 10 to 24/min; SpO₂ > 95%
- SBP > 90 without tachycardia or bradycardia
- GCS > 13

**Vulnerable population / risk for early deterioration** — patients with stable primary survey with:

- Chronic liver disease
- Chronic kidney disease
- Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus
- H/o fever with immune suppression
- Post-ictal patient
- Elderly, paediatric or pregnant patient
- Persistent vomiting / decreased urine output

#### GREEN

- Require minimum or OPD-based care
- Patients with stable primary survey & no risk factors for deterioration

## 2. Guide to succinct clinical documentation in ED

**Sample format for initial documentation in the Emergency Medicine case sheet**

Record the **date & time at which the patient is examined**, then:

> Case seen by Dr ABC *(write your name & designation)*

- Previous known co-morbid illness of the patient
- Chief complaint of the patient (with duration)

### Primary survey (with steps for stabilisation)

| Step | To document |
|---|---|
| **A — Airway** | Patent / threatened / obstructed → intervention |
| **B — Breathing** | RR; SpO₂; bilateral air entry |
| **C — Circulation** | Pulse rate /min (regular, irregular); BP; capillary refill time / peripheral perfusion |
| **D — Disability** | GCS (E, V, M); any focal neurologic deficit; pupil examination / meningeal signs / plantar reflexes |
| **E — Exposure** | Temperature; salient head-to-toe examination findings |

- Brief history as per the presenting complaint
- Relevant negative history
- Relevant focussed systemic examination findings

### POC investigations

- Write the investigation sent, with the **time at which sent**
- Mention salient investigation findings such as ECG, blood gas findings
- RBS

### Disposition and communication

- Instructions regarding shifting the patient to a specific area as per triage category (yellow / red) **and handover note**
- Consultations, if any informed, with the time at which informed

### Documentation of emergency treatment advised / given

Example: *1) Inj. Paracetamol 1 g i.v. stat*

- Number the drugs
- Mention the name of the medication, formulation, dose and route of administration clearly
- Mention if any other intervention done, such as NG lavage, Foley's catheterisation etc.

### Other specific documentation

Example: blood product arrangement, important investigation to be reviewed, etc.

> **If the patient has any known allergies to any specific drug, mention it on the front sheet in CAPITAL LETTERS.**

## 3. Assessment of a patient with Chest Pain

### Salient history to be taken

- **Site** of pain — localised or not
- **Onset & duration** of pain
- **Radiation** of pain: jaw, arm, epigastrium, neck, back, inter-scapular region
- **Aggravating factors:** exertion, post-prandial
- **Relieving factors:** rest, medication
- **Associated:** nausea, vomiting, sweating, palpitation, syncope, any limb weakness
- H/o recent surgery / immobilisation / any limb swelling / prolonged travel / trauma
- H/o fever / cough / expectoration, breathing difficulty / haemoptysis
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, malignancy
- **Medication history:** medications the patient is previously continuing, and any given at the referring healthcare facility

### Salient clinical examination

- Complete primary assessment
- Measure blood pressure in **both upper limbs**
- JVP, pedal oedema
- Chest auscultation to look for air entry, wheeze, crepitation
- Cardiac auscultation for any systolic murmur
- Quick neurologic assessment to look for limb weakness, slurring of speech

### Point-of-care investigations

*Investigations to be done based on probable clinical diagnosis. "\*" marked investigations to be discussed with the Senior Resident before proceeding.*

- **Expedite ECG (within 10 minutes of patient presentation to ED)**
- Point-of-care USG — lung, cardiac & vascular USG
- Baseline CBC & renal function test
- Blood gas (when indicated clinically)
- Troponin I\*
- D-dimer\*
- Chest X-ray

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Patient has associated profuse sweating / persistent vomiting
- Unstable primary assessment
- ECG showing ST elevation or depression in any leads

### Basic initial management

> **\*Always discuss with the Senior Resident on shift before proceeding with any of the following management.**
>
> **Shift to Red area if red flags present.**

Loading dose of medications:

- Tab Aspirin 325 mg
- Tab Clopidogrel 300 mg
- Tab Atorvastatin 80 mg
- Tab Sorbitrate 5 mg sublingual stat

*Refer to the "Medications in ED" section for contraindications and other details.*

> **Note:** The second page of each symptom approach is a brief guide to critical and emergent differentials in ED, which is **only for knowledge purpose**. **No critical treatment or intervention is to be initiated independently by the JR without informing the SR Emergency Medicine on shift.**

---

### Quick guide to important ED differentials of chest pain

#### Acute Coronary Syndrome

- Patient with central chest wall heaviness / dyspnoea / giddiness / *ghabrahat* / syncope
- Associated sweating, nausea, vomiting
- Exertional symptoms
- Radiation anywhere from jaw to epigastrium or either arm

**ECG:** new ST/T changes **OR** conduction blocks

- **STEMI:** ST elevation of minimum 1 mm in contiguous leads, except in V2–V3 where > 1.5 mm (female); > 2 mm (male)
  - ST elevation II, III, aVF → inferior wall STEMI
  - STE V1–V4 → anterior wall STEMI
  - STE I, aVL, V5, V6 → lateral wall STEMI
- **NSTEMI:** new onset ST elevation, depression or T inversion (not fitting into above criteria) **OR** elevated troponin I
- **Unstable angina:** typical angina symptoms lasting > 20 minutes, crescendo pattern, with no new ECG changes and **no** troponin elevation

Troponin elevation > 99th percentile (see reference value as per the test).
**ECHO:** RWMA (regional wall motion abnormality).

**Expedite initial treatment: ECG within 10 mins. Window period: 6–12 hours.**

#### Aortic Dissection

- Sudden onset chest pain radiating to back (inter-scapular region)
- "Chest pain plus" syndrome (chest pain with limb weakness, chest pain with stroke, limb ischemia)
- BP / pulse deficit in two limbs
- H/o hypertension / previous aortic disorders
- **Diagnosis:** CT aortogram

#### Boerhaave Syndrome

- Spontaneous oesophageal rupture
- Sudden onset sharp substernal pain
- H/o severe retching, vomiting, or manoeuvres with sudden negative intrathoracic pressure
- Tachycardia, dyspnoea, sweating, fever
- Hamman's crunch (audible crepitus with heart beat) — rare finding on examination
- Pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, pleural effusion, subcutaneous emphysema
- **Diagnosis:** CECT thorax with oral contrast

> Management of the below diagnoses is explained briefly in the section on Dyspnoea.

#### Massive Pulmonary Embolism

- Patient with recent surgery, limb immobilisation, deep vein thrombosis, limb swelling or malignancy
- Sudden onset breathlessness / chest pain / syncope
- Hypoxia with clear chest on auscultation / POCUS
- **ECG:** sinus tachycardia, T inversion in V1, V2, V3 or inferior chest leads, S1Q3T3 sign
- **ECHO:** RA/RV dilatation or RV dysfunction

#### Cardiac Tamponade

- H/o trauma, mediastinal or lung malignancy, CKD, TB, recent MI
- Raised JVP / distended neck veins / hypotension
- **POCUS:** circumferential fluid collection around the heart with RA/RV diastolic collapse
- Get wide-bore i.v. access & i.v. crystalloid bolus
- Prepare for pericardiocentesis

#### Tension Pneumothorax

- H/o trauma, COPD, bullous lung disease, TB
- Sudden onset SOB
- Tracheal deviation, unilateral absence of breath sounds, hypotension, raised JVP, distended neck veins
- **POCUS:** absent lung sliding (barcode sign on M-mode)
- **Emergency treatment:** needle thoracentesis

#### Lobar Pneumonia

- H/o fever + chills, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, haemoptysis
- Fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, crepitation, decreased breath sounds
- **POCUS:** focal B-lines, pleural shredding, hepatisation, syn-pneumonic effusion
- **CXR:** e/o lobar radio-opacity with air bronchogram, syn-pneumonic effusion

## 4. Assessment of a patient with Suspected Stroke

### Salient history to be taken

- **Typical symptoms — FAST**
  - **F**acial deviation (asymmetrical face while smiling / clenching)
  - **A**rm drift / limb weakness
  - **S**peech abnormality (slurring / aphasia)
  - **T**ime to call SR Neurology
- **Atypical symptoms:** numbness, dizziness / vertigo, confusion / altered sensorium, visual disturbance, severe headache
- Baseline neurological status, **time last seen at normal / at baseline**
- **Onset & duration** of symptoms
- Progression or resolution of symptoms
- **Associated:** headache, vomiting, seizure, incontinence
- H/o trauma, intracranial haemorrhage
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, chronic kidney disease, malignancy
- **Medication history:** anticoagulants, any other medicines / medicines given at referring facility

### Salient clinical examination

- **Check random blood sugar**
- Complete primary assessment
- Document GCS and the **3 Ps**
  - **P**upil: size & reaction
  - **P**ower: B/L upper & lower limbs
  - **P**lantar response
- In case of ongoing vertigo / cerebellar symptoms, perform **HINTS plus** examination

### Point-of-care investigations

- RBS
- *Inform SR Neurology ASAP in suspected stroke after ruling out hypoglycaemia*
- CBC & renal function test
- Coagulation profile
- **NCCT head → stroke imaging**
- Rapid antigen test for COVID-19 (expedite for CT purpose)
- ECG & chest X-ray — can be deferred till after CT
- POC ECHO: if cardio-embolic stroke / dissection is suspected
- Blood gas (if indicated in an unstable patient)

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Unstable primary assessment: e.g. BP > 185/110, SpO₂ < 94%
- Patient is unresponsive / difficult to arouse / ongoing seizure
- Anisocoria, extensor posturing

### Basic initial management

> **\*Always discuss with the Senior Resident on shift before proceeding with any of the following management.**

- IV cannula: preferably in right arm (20G or larger)
- If RBS < 70 mg/dL → Inj. 50% dextrose 50 mL IV & reassess
- **Inform SR EM & SR Neurology ASAP & shift patient for CT**

**Targets to achieve**

- SR Neurology review & NCCT head **by 25 minutes** of arrival (*always NCCT first to r/o haemorrhagic stroke*)
- Glucose = 140–180
- BP ≤ 185/110
- Fibrinolytic therapy started **by 60 minutes** of arrival, if eligible
- Endovascular therapy **by 6 hours** of arrival, if eligible

---

### Quick guide to important interventions in stroke

#### BP control

- Labetalol 10–20 mg IV over 1–2 min (repeat once), **OR**
- Nicardipine IV infusion at 5 mg/h, titrate up by 2.5 mg/h every 5–15 min (max 15 mg/h)

#### HINTS plus examination

- **H**ead impulse test — corrective saccade **absent**
- **N**ystagmus — multidirectional
- **T**est of **S**kew — vertical skew
- **Plus** — new onset hearing loss (check by rubbing fingers next to each ear)

If the above are true → **central cause of vertigo** (r/o stroke).

#### UMN vs LMN facial palsy

UMN = stroke; LMN = e.g. Bell's palsy.

Features of **UMN** palsy:

- Forehead wrinkling on upward gaze — present
- Ability to completely close the eye — present

#### Fibrinolytic therapy

- Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg IV (max 90 mg): 10% over 1 min, remaining over 60 min, **OR**
- Tenecteplase 0.5 mg/kg IV (max 50 mg) as bolus over 5–10 sec

**Inclusion criteria (within 3 hrs of symptom onset)**

- Age ≥ 18
- Measurable diagnosis of ischemic stroke

**Inclusion criteria (within 4.5 hrs of symptom onset)**

- Above criteria **AND**
- Age ≤ 80
- NIHSS ≤ 25
- No h/o uncontrolled DM, prior stroke, anticoagulant use
- Imaging shows ≤ 1/3rd MCA territory involved

**Contraindications for fibrinolysis**

- Not meeting inclusion criteria
- Acute or prior IC bleed
- Severe head trauma / ischemic stroke in past 3 months
- Intracranial / intraspinal surgery in past 3 months
- GI bleed / malignancy in past 3 weeks
- BP > 185/110 despite therapy
- Platelet < 1 lakh or INR > 1.7

#### Endovascular therapy

Stent retriever or mechanical thrombectomy.

**Inclusion criteria (must meet ALL)**

- Age ≥ 18
- NIHSS ≥ 6
- ASPECTS ≥ 6
- Pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale score 0–1
- Stroke due to ICA / proximal MCA occlusion
- Fibrinolysis received within 4.5 hrs of onset
- Groin puncture for endovascular therapy initiated within 6 hrs of onset

## 5. Assessment of a patient with Shortness of Breath (SOB)

### Salient history to be taken

- **Onset:** sudden (think pulmonary embolism, AMI, pneumothorax) vs progressive (other)
- **Duration:** acute or chronic
- **Severity assessment:** SOB on exertion / limitation of daily activities / at rest
- Positional change like orthopnoea, PND
- **Associated symptoms:** fever, chest pain (central or lateral), diaphoresis, musical sound on breathing, cough, haemoptysis, pedal oedema, unilateral limb swelling, recurrent vomiting, abdominal pain
- **Previous H/o:** similar episode, any seasonal variation, any recent surgery, immobilisation, prolonged travel
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, malignancy, tuberculosis
- **Medication history:** medications previously continuing & any given at the referring healthcare facility (particularly inhalers, cardiac medications, painkillers, diuretics)
- **Personal history:** smoking, working near *chulha* or furnaces, working in mines

### Salient clinical examination

- Complete primary assessment
- Signs of airway obstruction, use of accessory muscles
- Raised JVP, pedal oedema, calf tenderness
- Chest auscultation: air entry (any unilateral decrease), wheeze, crepitation
- Cardiac auscultation for any systolic murmur
- Subcutaneous emphysema in neck and/or chest, tracheal deviation
- Evidence of chest or neck trauma

### Point-of-care investigations

*Investigations to be done based on probable clinical diagnosis.*

- ECG
- Arterial blood gas (always note the supplemental oxygen rate if given)
- Point-of-care lung, cardiac & vascular USG
- Baseline CBC, RFT
- Chest X-ray (**if patient is unstable — never send the patient out of ED**)
- Testing for COVID-19
- Troponin I\*
- INR, D-dimer\*
- NT-proBNP\*
- Blood culture
- RBS and urine ketones\*

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Signs of airway obstruction — stridor, pooling of oral secretion, gross lips/tongue swelling, neck mass or injury
- Severe tachypnea (RR > 30/min), hypoxia (SpO₂ < 94%)
- Associated with chest pain, profuse sweating, altered sensorium
- Unilateral air entry absent, use of accessory respiratory muscles
- Silent chest on auscultation

### Basic initial management

> **\*Always discuss with the Senior Resident on shift before proceeding with any of the following management.**
>
> **Shift to Red area immediately if red flags present.**

- Shift all patients of acute SOB to monitored areas and connect to the cardiac monitor and SpO₂ probe
- Provide supplemental oxygen to target a goal SpO₂ ≥ 94% (for COPD patients target SpO₂ 88–92%)
- Get an intravenous access (preferably wide bore) and send all the samples
- Manage as per the following table of differentials

### Differential diagnosis of SOB — signs, tests and initial treatment

#### Pulmonary causes of SOB

**Airway obstruction**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o sudden choking, neck/face trauma, depressed consciousness; stridor, pooling of oral secretions, low GCS (≤ 8)
- *Diagnostic tests:* no diagnostic test needed
- *Initial treatment:* head tilt–chin lift / jaw thrust; oral suction if secretions; connect to supplemental oxygen; prepare for definitive airway

**Pneumothorax (PTX)**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o trauma, COPD, lung malignancy, TB, connective tissue disease; sudden onset SOB; tracheal deviation, unilateral absence of breath sounds, hypotension, raised JVP
- *Diagnostic tests:* **if patient is haemodynamically unstable, DO NOT send the patient for CXR**; POCUS — lung scan
- *Initial treatment:* supplemental oxygen; tension PTX (unstable) — needle decompression (use 16" IV cannula) in the 5th intercostal space in the anterior axillary line; plan for ICD placement

**Acute exacerbation of asthma and COPD**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:*
  - Asthma — h/o similar episode in past, seasonal variation, allergy, MDI use, family history of dry cough
  - COPD — known smoker, similar episode in past, MDI use, dry cough
  - Signs — audible wheeze, bilateral wheeze, tripod positioning, pursing of lips, air hunger, use of accessory muscles
  - Features of precipitants like LRTI, cardiac ischemia
- *Diagnostic tests:* POCUS / CXR to r/o LRTI, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema; blood gas — acute or acute-on-chronic respiratory acidosis (pH < 7.35, pCO₂ > 40 mmHg, HCO₃ normal or slightly increased)
- *Initial treatment:*
  - SpO₂ target: 88–92%
  - Salbutamol: MDI (2–4 puffs) or nebulisation (1 respule), q20 min × 3
  - Ipratropium: MDI (2 puffs) or neb (2 respules), q20 min × 3
  - Adequate hydration
  - If precipitant is LRTI: give antibiotics as per SR advice (see Pneumonia section)
  - \*Oral / IV steroid (Inj. prednisolone 1 mg/kg)
  - Plan for NIV vs intubation — SR decision

**Pneumonia**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o fever + chills, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, haemoptysis; h/o travel; exposure to COVID patient; fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, crepitation, decreased breath sounds
- *Diagnostic tests:* ABG if patient is hypoxic (always note the FiO₂ or rate of O₂ supplied, to find P/F ratio); CBC — change in TLC; POCUS / CXR — consolidation; **send COVID CBNAAT/RT-PCR**; send blood cultures prior to start of antibiotics; **CRB-65 score** — 1 point each for confusion, RR > 30, BP < 90/60, age > 65 yrs
- *Initial treatment:*
  - Red triaged — shift to "Isolation" Red area
  - Supplemental O₂, target SpO₂ ≥ 94%
  - Start antibiotics:
    - CURB-65 < 1: oral — Tab Amoxi-clav 625 mg PO TDS + Tab Azithromycin 500 mg PO OD × 5 days
    - CURB-65 ≥ 2: IV — Inj. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV BD (SR decision to escalate) + Tab/Inj. Azithromycin 500 mg OD
  - Adequate IV fluids / oral hydration
  - SR discretion for starting dexamethasone

**Pleural effusion**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o TB, malignancy, CKD, CLD, CHF, pneumonia; unilateral decreased breath sounds
- *Diagnostic tests:* POCUS — lung scan; CXR — pleural effusion
- *Initial treatment:* if patient is in respiratory distress, shift to Red area and plan for thoracentesis

**Anaphylaxis**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* sudden onset SOB with exposure to likely/known allergen; any recent drug or new diet change; lips/tongue swelling, skin rash (urticaria), wheezing, pain abdomen, vomiting, giddiness
- *Diagnostic tests:* no diagnostic test needed
- *Initial treatment:*
  - Adequate O₂ and IVF
  - **Inj. Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM, anterolateral thigh** — repeat if necessary
  - Neb SABA + ipratropium (as described in asthma section)
  - Inj. Ranitidine 50 mg IV
  - Inj. Hydrocortisone 250–500 mg IV

#### Cardiac causes

**Acute heart failure and pulmonary oedema (incl. non-cardiac causes of fluid overload)**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o SOB on exertion, any heart disease, previous similar history, PND; raised JVP, bilateral crepitation, cardiac murmurs, pedal oedema; if associated with chest pain follow the chest pain section; check non-adherence to medications; h/o RHD and valve replacement; h/o CKD, haemodialysis schedule, CLD
- *Diagnostic tests:* ECG for ACS, arrhythmias; lung POCUS — B/L B-profile; ECHO — EF measurement, valve motion, cardiac tamponade; blood gas — oxygenation, acidosis, K⁺; CXR — bilateral hilar prominence (bat-wing pattern); BNP / NT-proBNP raised; send INR (in RHD patients); RFT, LFT; USG KUB (AKI vs CKD)
- *Initial treatment:*
  - Head-end elevation and O₂ as needed
  - Decide according to BP:
    - **> 140/90 mmHg:** Inj. Furosemide 40 mg IV; NIV and NTG infusion as per SR
    - **Normal BP:** Inj. Furosemide 40 mg IV; NIV as per SR advice
    - **< 90/60 mmHg:** follow SR advice
  - If suspected LRTI: start antibiotics
  - Management of uraemia as per SR advice

**Pulmonary embolism (PE)**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o chest pain, SOB, syncope, prolonged immobilisation, malignancy, unilateral leg swelling, previous h/o PE. **One important differential of SOB WITH CLEAR CHEST.** Tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxia, suspected DVT
- *Diagnostic tests:* ECG — sinus tachycardia, ST/T changes, RBBB/RAD; POCUS — RA, RV dilatation, RV dysfunction; CXR usually normal; send CBC, coagulation, RFT; USG Doppler of lower limb to r/o DVT; CTPA and D-dimer\*
- *Initial treatment:*
  - O₂ as per requirement; follow SR instructions
  - If planned to start anticoagulation: Inj. Enoxaparin 0.6 mL s.c. BD **OR** Inj. UFH 80 U/kg IV stat, f/b infusion @ 18 U/kg/hr

**Cardiac tamponade**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o trauma, mediastinal or lung malignancy, CKD, TB; raised JVP, hypotension
- *Diagnostic tests:* ECG — low voltage QRS, electrical alternans; echocardiography
- *Initial treatment:* shift the patient to Red area; follow SR instructions

#### Metabolic and others

**Metabolic acidosis**

- *Suggestive symptoms and signs:* H/o diabetes, oral antidiabetics/insulin non-adherence; polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, abdominal pain, recurrent vomiting; any signs and symptoms of infection; h/o CKD + haemodialysis
- *Diagnostic tests:*
  - Metabolic acidosis: pH < 7.35, HCO₃ < 22, pCO₂ decreased
  - DKA: RBS > 250 mg/dL; VBG pH < 7.3, HCO₃ < 18; urine ketones positive
  - Send electrolytes (K⁺), RFT
  - USG KUB (AKI vs CKD, pyelonephritis)
- *Initial treatment:*
  - Primary survey and stabilisation
  - For DKA: 2 wide-bore IV lines, IVF (1 L/hr RL); check serum K⁺ (VBG) and follow SR advice to start insulin + KCl infusion; connect to ECG & put Foley's (urine output)
  - Follow SR instruction for managing metabolic acidosis, e.g. starting NaHCO₃ infusion

  - H/o toxin / drug overdose / bite / sting
  - H/o trauma
  - H/o cough / dyspnoea / dysuria / loose stools
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, CKD, CLD, COPD, malignancy, tuberculosis, psychiatric illness, previous stroke, thyroid disorder, other

  - Psychiatric / thyroid medication

- Complete primary assessment **including temperature**
- Focal neurologic deficit
- Pupillary examination
- Plantar reflex
- Meningeal signs
- Head-to-toe & focussed systemic examination

  - If any other focus of infection
- Focussed neurological examination including reflexes (DTR)

- RBS
- CBC — infection? low platelet?
- RFT, serum electrolytes, bilirubin
- Blood gas — acidosis, initial electrolytes, lactate, anion gap

- Extensor posturing / anisocoria on pupillary examination

> **\*Always discuss with the Senior Resident on shift before proceeding with any of the following management.**
>
> **Shift to Red area if red flags present.**

  - Inj. Dextrose 50% 50 mL i.v. stat
  - Inj. Thiamine 100 mg i.v. (if h/o regular ethanol use / poor nutrition status)

- \*Antibiotics in meningitis dose (if meningitis suspected)
- Anti-hepatic encephalopathy measures

### Brief initial approach to seizure in ED

#### Salient history

- Onset of seizure, number of episodes, interval between episodes
- **Semiology** — description of the seizure

- Inj. Phenytoin 10–20 mg/kg BW (max 1 g)

- Tab. Phenytoin 100 mg TDS / 300 mg HS

- Tab. Clonazepam 0.5 mg / 1 mg OD/BD

## 8. Initial assessment of a patient with UGI Bleed

### Salient history to be taken

- **Quantity** — duration, number of episodes per day, amount of bleed per episode
- **Colour** — coffee / dark / fresh
- **Differentiate between haematemesis and haemoptysis**
- **Malaena** — black, foul smelling, sticky, associated with loose stools
- **Associated:** haematochezia without clots, altered sensorium, abdominal pain, fever, decreased urine output, other bleeding manifestations
- Previous history of haematemesis and treatment if any
- Chest pain, epigastric pain

**History for aetiology**

- H/o jaundice, abdominal pain, fever, vomiting, history of CLD, alcohol intake, blood transfusion → **CLD**
- H/o medication use — NSAID abuse, antiplatelet (aspirin, clopidogrel), anticoagulant, steroids → **peptic ulcer disease**
- H/o vomiting and retching, binge drinking, DKA → **Mallory-Weiss tear**
- H/o loss of weight, loss of appetite, abdominal distension, anorexia → **gastric malignancy**
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, chronic liver disease, coronary artery disease, GI malignancy

### Salient examination

- Complete primary survey
- Per abdomen: ascites, tenderness
- CNS: GCS, focal deficit
- RS: decreased breath sounds, localised crepitation
- Signs of CLD if aetiology of bleed is unknown

### POC investigations

- **Blood grouping and cross match**
- CBC — Hb, PLT, TLC
- Blood gas — lactate, base deficit
- LFT, RFT
- PT/INR
- ECG if age > 50 y, h/o CAD
- USG abdomen — if previous CLD is undiagnosed (**never send an unstable patient for USG abdomen**)
- CBNAAT — COVID-19 testing for UGI scopy

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Unstable primary assessment
- Massive / ongoing UGI bleed

### Basic initial management

> Shift to Red area in case of red flag signs. **\*Inform SR about the case.**

- Get wide-bore i.v. access (18 or 16 G)
- IVF RL 1 L bolus (if patient in shock); additional fluid based on patient profile

**Variceal bleed (CLD)**

- Inj. Somatostatin 500 µg stat f/b 250 µg/hr infusion **OR**
- Inj. Terlipressin 1–2 mg IV q4h
- Inj. Pantoprazole 40 mg IV

**Non-variceal bleed (others)**

- Inj. Pantoprazole 80 mg stat f/b 8 mg/hr infusion
- NG lavage (*do not perform without supervision if patient has compromised airway or is in altered sensorium*)
- Antibiotics: Inj. Cefotaxime 2 g IV ATD

**Blood products**

- Activate **Massive Transfusion Protocol** if patient has ongoing GI bleed with haemorrhagic shock (*discuss with SR*)
- Transfuse PRBC if: active bleed, or Hb < 7
- RDP if PLT < 50,000 / massive bleed
- FFP if INR > 1.5 / massive bleed

> **Inform SR Gastro for UGI scopy.**

>
> **Shift to Red area if red flag signs.**

- Treat co-existing LRTI if present with antibiotics

### Quick guide on haemoptysis, haematemesis and oro-nasal bleed in ED

#### Haematemesis vs haemoptysis

| Feature | Haematemesis | Haemoptysis |
|---|---|---|
| Typical background | CLD / peptic ulcer | TB / CA lung |
| Colour | Dark brown / coffee colour | Fresh blood |
| Sequence | Vomiting precedes blood | Cough precedes blood |
| Frothy | Not frothy | Frothy (due to air) |
| Melena | Present | Absent |
| Food particles | Present | Absent |
| pH | Acidic | Alkaline |

#### Oro-nasal bleed

- Shift to Red area if compromised primary survey
- Wide-bore i.v. access; arrange blood, CBC, coagulation studies
- **If patient has epistaxis:**
  - Anterior epistaxis: external pressure by pinching the nose × 15–20 mins
  - Topical phenylephrine / oxymetazoline
  - Posterior epistaxis: check BP, treat hypertension if BP > 160/90
  - If persistent bleeding — anterior / posterior nasal packing / cauterization
- **If oral bleed:** check source — trauma, tumour, tooth. Topical vasoconstrictor / pressure / haemostat — inform SR on shift

#### Management of UGI bleed case post-endoscopy

- Repeat CBC if patient has haemodynamic instability or ongoing GI bleed; if not, at 12 hours post endoscopy
- **Variceal bleed**
  - High-grade varices: somatostatin infusion for 72 hours
  - Low-grade varices: somatostatin infusion for 24–48 hours
- **Non-variceal bleed**
  - High-grade ulcers: NPO for 24 hours; pantoprazole infusion / boluses for 3–5 days
  - Low-grade ulcers: discharge with oral pantoprazole 40 mg BD
- **Re-bleed / failure of endoscopy**
  - > 100 mL bleed in 2 hours
  - Unstable vitals
  - Haemoglobin drop by > 3 g/dL
  - Haematocrit drop > 9%
  - Monitor vitals every 2 hourly

#### Haemoptysis — key points

- Blood loss of 100–600 mL in 24 hours
- **Practical definition of massive haemoptysis in ED:** any amount of haemoptysis that causes airway, breathing or haemodynamic compromise
- Requires imaging (MDCT / CT bronchial angiogram) → aetiology evaluation → intervention (radiology / CTVS)
- Minor haemoptysis with recurrent episodes, deranged coagulation, or anticoagulant medications requires ED evaluation & observation
- Other cases of minor haemoptysis may be reassured and referred to OPD after explaining warning signs

- **Suicidal / accidental / homicidal**
- **Symptoms:** non-specific (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain) & specific toxidromes
- Previous psychiatric / co-morbid illness
- **Medication history:** medications previously continuing & any given at the referring healthcare facility

**Common agents encountered in AIIMS ER**

- Corrosives
- Drugs (benzodiazepines / PCM / antipsychotics)
- Rodenticides (superwarfarin / aluminium phosphide)
- Cypermethrin (All Out)
- Organophosphates

### Salient clinical examination

- Complete primary assessment
- Check consciousness and pupils
- Particular odours associated with certain intoxicants
- Examine skin and mucous membranes → discolouration and hydration
- Examine CNS focusing on muscle tone, clonus, hyperpyrexia
- Evidence of drug abuse

### Point-of-care investigations

*Investigations to be done based on probable clinical diagnosis.*

- ECG
- RBS
- Baseline CBC & renal function test
- Blood gas
- Urine toxicology
- Chest X-ray & abdominal X-ray (in corrosive ingestions)
- Osmolar gap (urine / serum)

### 🚩 Red flags

- Unstable primary assessment
- Patient with persistent vomiting

### Treatment / disposition plan

> **\*Always discuss with the Senior Resident on shift before proceeding with any of the following management.**

- Oxygen if SpO₂ < 94%
- IV crystalloid bolus — if SBP < 90
- Inj. Dextrose 50% 50 mL i.v. if RBS < 70 mg/dL, **with**
- Inj. Thiamine 100 mg i.v.
- Treat as per the toxin (see toxidromes below)
- **Do not do lavage for patients with corrosive ingestion**
- For patients with persistent vomiting, altered sensorium, or unstable airway, do an NG lavage **only under SR supervision**
- Every patient coming with a history of toxin ingestion is a **Medico-Legal Case (MLC)**. The same has to be made and countersigned by the Senior Resident.

### Quick guide to important toxidromes

#### Cholinergic (organophosphates and carbamates)

- Salivation
- Lacrimation
- Diaphoresis
- Vomiting, urination
- Defecation
- Bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, bradycardia
- Fasciculation
- Miosis
- Seizures

**Antidote:** Atropine (initial dose 0.6–2 mg i.v.)

#### Anticholinergic

- Altered mental status
- Mydriasis
- Dry flushed skin
- Urinary retention
- Decreased bowel sounds
- Hyperthermia
- Dry mucous membranes
- Seizures & arrhythmias

#### Sympathomimetic

- Agitation
- Tachycardia
- Hypertension
- Hyperpyrexia
- Diaphoresis
- Seizures
- Mydriasis
- Acute coronary syndrome

#### Opioid

- Hypoventilation
- CNS depression
- Hypothermia
- Bradycardia
- Miosis

**Antidote:** Naloxone (initial dose 2 mg i.v., **OR** 0.4 mg i.v. in chronic drug user)

#### Snake bite

- **Neurotoxic**
  - Look for signs of limb weakness / diplopia / ptosis
  - Do a single breath count (< 10)
- **Haemotoxic**
  - Whole blood clotting time (> 20 mins)
  - Signs of obvious bleeding

**Antidote:** Anti-snake venom (only if signs of envenomation) — dose: 10 vials ASV in 500 mL 5% dextrose over 1 hour

#### Corrosive injury

- Look for airway compromise (hoarseness of voice / stridor / difficulty breathing)
- Signs of perforation (guarding / rigidity / haematemesis)

### Other common antidotes

| Toxin | Antidote |
|---|---|
| Paracetamol | N-acetyl cysteine |
| Sodium channel blocker toxicity | Sodium bicarbonate |
| Calcium channel blockers / β-blockers toxicity | Glucagon |
| Local anaesthetic toxicity | IV lipid emulsion 20% |
| Oxidizing toxins (e.g. nitrites, benzocaine, sulphonamides) | Methylene blue |
| Cyanide | Hydroxocobalamin |
| Benzodiazepine toxicity | Flumazenil |

### N-acetyl cysteine dose

**Intravenous** (for an average 60 kg person)

- 150 mg/kg in 200 mL 5% dextrose i.v. over 1 hour (9 g in 200 mL 5% D)
- 50 mg/kg in 500 mL 5% D over 4 hours (3 g in 500 mL 5% D @ 125 mL/hr)
- 100 mg/kg in 1 L 5% D over 16 hrs (3 g in 500 mL 5% D @ 60 mL/hr)

**Oral**

- Loading dose: 140 mg/kg PO stat
- Maintenance: start after 4 hrs — 70 mg/kg PO q4h × 17 doses

## 13. Approach to an Unresponsive patient

1. **Unresponsive patient** → shift the patient to the **RED AREA**
2. **Call for SR or any senior JR help**
3. Look for **breathing** (normal vs abnormal) & **pulse rate** (one-side carotid pulse check for 5 to 10 seconds) **simultaneously**

### If breathing and pulse are normal

- Check RBS (if < 60 mg/dL, give Inj. Dextrose 50%, 50 mL, IV stat)
- Primary survey and management
- Take detailed history (any intoxication, seizure, etc.)
- Focussed neurological examination including pupils, and other systemic examination
- Manage as per the "Altered Sensorium" section

### If pulse present but breathing abnormal

- **Open airway:** head tilt–chin lift / jaw thrust manoeuvre
- **Provide rescue breaths:** using bag-valve-mask (appropriate sized mask, adequate seal by E-C technique) — 1 breath every 6 seconds
- **Plan for further management** as per SR instruction

### If pulse absent

1. **ACTIVATE CODE BLUE** (informing telephone operator in the counter)
2. **BEGIN HIGH QUALITY CPR** — chest compression and airway management

**Chest compressor**

- Site: lower half of the sternum
- Hands: interlocked, elbow straight
- Rate: 100–120/min
- Depth: 5–6 cm
- Allow adequate recoil
- Do not interrupt CPR

**Airway manager**

- No advanced airway: give 2 breaths after each 30 chest compressions
- Advanced airway in place: one breath every 6 seconds
- Correct volume: visible chest rise

**Connect to defibrillator** as soon as possible

- **Shockable rhythm (pulseless VT or VF):** give DC shock (energy 200 J), then continue CPR for 2 minutes
- **Non-shockable (PEA or asystole):** continue CPR

**Meanwhile**

- One doctor should get an IV access
- Send VBG (for acidosis and K⁺) and get the USG machine (to r/o 5H & 5T)
- Order the nurse to give Inj. Adrenaline 1 mg IV stat with 20 mL saline push and raising the hand
- Adequate residents for change of chest compressor after 2 minutes of CPR
- Rhythm (± pulse) check every 2 minutes of CPR
- Inj. Adrenaline to repeat every 4 minutes
- **Follow the instructions of the team leader**

### Reversible causes of cardiac arrest (5H & 5T)

**5H**

- Hypovolemia
- Hypoxia
- Hydrogen ion excess (acidosis)
- Hypo/hyperkalemia
- Hypothermia

**5T**

- Tension pneumothorax (PTX)
- Tamponade, cardiac
- Thrombosis, cardiac (AMI)
- Thrombosis, pulmonary
- Toxins

> **Utilisation of POCUS (point-of-care ultrasound) & blood gas with electrolytes (VBG) will help in finding the reversible causes.**

### Important drug dosage and treatment of the cause

- **Epinephrine:** 1 mg IV stat with 20 mL saline push and raising the hand
- **Amiodarone:** 1st 300 mg IV; 2nd 150 mg IV
- **Hyperkalemic arrest:** Inj. Calcium gluconate 10%, 30 mL, IV push
- **Metabolic acidosis:** Inj. NaHCO₃ (1.5 mEq/kg) — 100 mEq, IV push
- **Antidote** for suspected toxins
- **PTX** — needle decompression
- **AMI** — as per team leader instruction (urgent cath lab activation + thrombolysis)
- **Pulmonary embolism** — team leader's instruction, thrombolysis
- **Tamponade** — pericardiocentesis

## 14. Assessment of a patient with Chronic Liver Disease (CLD) in ED

### Salient history to be taken

- Duration of chronic liver disease
- **Aetiology (if already known)**
  - Ethanol, viral, autoimmune
  - Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NASH / NAFLD)
  - Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)
  - Budd-Chiari syndrome / HVOTO
- **Current symptoms**
  - UGI bleed, melena, constipation
  - Fever, abdomen pain / distension
  - Worsening jaundice / other bleeding
  - Cough / shortness of breath / syncope
  - Altered sleep / sensorium / seizure
  - Loose stools / decreased urine output
- **Past & treatment history**
  - Medications patient is taking
  - Previous UGI scopy / colonoscopy
  - Previous episodes of similar illness
  - H/o portal venous thrombosis / anticoagulant medication
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, hypertension, CAD, hepatitis B (HBV), HCV

### Salient clinical examination

Complete primary survey. Head-to-toe examination: pallor, oedema.

**Stigmata of CLD**

- Frontal alopecia, icterus
- Parotid enlargement (raised ear lobule)
- Spider angioma (telangiectasia)
- Loss of axillary hair, gynaecomastia
- Palmar erythema, asterixis
- Dupuytren's contracture
- Ascites, caput medusae
- Testicular atrophy

**Other:** ecchymosis / bleeding manifestation

- **P/A:** ascites — shifting dullness / fluid thrill; organomegaly, bowel sounds, scrotal oedema, hernia orifices
- **Chest:** decreased breath sounds / crackles
- **CVS:** any systolic murmur?
- **CNS:** focal neurologic deficit, pupils, meningeal signs
- **Per rectal examination** — for melena (if there is diagnostic dilemma)

### Point-of-care investigations

- **Blood grouping & cross match**
- CBC — Hb, PLT, TLC
- Blood gas — lactate; RBS
- LFT, RFT + serum electrolytes
- PT/INR/aPTT
- **POCUS:** fluid status of patient, ascites, pleural effusion / any e/o LRTI
- ECG if age > 50 y, h/o CAD
- **Ascitic fluid (SBP):** TC/DC, protein, albumin & culture

**Imaging**

- **USG abdomen** — if previous CLD is undiagnosed; portal venous thrombosis
- **X-ray abdomen** erect / lateral decubitus — if perforation / obstruction suspected
- **NCCT brain** — to r/o coagulopathy-related bleed if anisocoria or focal deficit present
- CBNAAT — COVID-19 testing for UGI scopy

> *Never send an unstable patient for imaging or USG abdomen.*

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Unstable primary survey
- Active GI bleed
- Hb < 4 g/dL
- Metabolic acidosis or high lactate on blood gas

### Brief initial management

- Get wide-bore i.v. access (18 or 16 G)
- IVF RL 1 L bolus (if patient in shock); additional fluid based on patient profile
- Inj. Dextrose 50% 50 mL i.v. if RBS < 70 mg/dL
- Inj. Thiamine 100 mg IV
- NG lavage (to check for UGI bleed / active GI bleed)

*For detailed management of UGI bleed, see section 8. For management of other CLD complications, see below.*

## 15. Assessment of a patient with Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) in ED

### Salient history to be taken

- Duration & stage of CKD
- **Present symptoms**
  - Shortness of breath
  - Chest pain, palpitation, syncope
  - Headache, seizure, limb weakness
  - Fever, cough, expectoration
  - Dysuria, pain abdomen, decreased urine output
  - Worsening limb / body swelling
  - Vomiting, loose stools
- Symptom onset, progression & associated features
- If patient has undergone **renal transplant**: if yes, when? Immunosuppressive medications & any complications
- **Haemodialysis-related history**
  - If dialysis initiated, if yes, when?
  - Number of dialyses per week
  - Last dialysis & if any events
  - Access for dialysis — fistula / catheter
- Medication history & baseline creatinine
- Blood transfusions if any
- **Co-morbid illness:** diabetes, HTN, old CVA, CAD, any autoimmune illness, malignancy, retroviral illness

### Salient clinical examination

- Complete primary assessment
- Head-to-toe examination: pallor, oedema, JVP, oral cavity & skin for any lesion / infective foci including dialysis access site, asterixis
- Chest auscultation — crepitation, wheeze, dullness on percussion
- Cardiac auscultation for S3, pericardial friction rub, muffled heart sounds
- Quick neurological examination — altered sensorium, focal deficit, meningeal signs
- P/A for ascites, organomegaly, renal angle tenderness

### Point-of-care investigations

- CBC — Hb, platelet, TLC
- RBS
- Blood gas — acidosis, K⁺ levels
- RFT + serum electrolytes
- ECG — hyperkalaemia or ACS
- POCUS — lung for fluid overload, pleural effusion, LRTI, pericardial effusion, LV function
- \*Blood group + cross match
- \*Urine microscopy — UTI
- \*Blood / urine culture — sepsis

\**If indicated*

**Imaging**

- USG abdomen: if dilemma between AKI & CKD; e/o pyelonephritis, obstructive uropathy
- CXR: LRTI / pleural effusion

> *Avoid sampling / cannulation / BP cuff attachment on the limb with a functional AV fistula.*

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Unstable primary assessment
- Tall tented T waves on ECG
- Severe metabolic acidosis or potassium > 5.9 on blood gas

### Basic initial management

- Obtain IV access (preferably right upper limb)
- *Shift to monitored area / red area if red flags present*
- Supplemental oxygen if SpO₂ < 94%; initiate NIV if severely hypoxic with high work of breathing
- POCUS-guided IV fluids (*do not give IV fluid boluses without consulting SR*)
- Anti-hypertensive medication & IV diuretics (*consult SR*)
- If K⁺ > 5.5 — anti-hyperkalaemia measures (see section 17)

**Notes**

1. Blood transfusion in CKD patients is to be done under strict monitoring, preferably in red areas.
2. Post renal transplant patients, being immunocompromised, may be relatively asymptomatic and hence require careful examination before final disposition.

### Diagnosis & management of common CLD and CKD related complications in ED

#### Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE)

**West Haven classification of HE grades**

| Grade | Features |
|---|---|
| 1 | Shortened attention span, reversal of sleep cycle, euphoria/anxiety |
| 2 | Lethargy/apathy, minimal disorientation for time or place, asterixis |
| 3 | Somnolence / semi-stupor, gross disorientation, responsive to verbal stimuli |
| 4 | Comatose, unresponsive to verbal / noxious stimuli |

**Management of hepatic encephalopathy**

- Syp. Lactulose 30 mL q4–8h PO or via NG tube
- Tab. Rifaximin 550 mg PO TDS
- Correct electrolyte imbalance if any (hypokalaemia or hyponatremia)
- Lactulose enema PR (if constipation not resolved)
- Treat precipitating cause / infection

#### Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)

- Fever, abdomen pain, chronic ascites
- Worsening jaundice, altered mental status, decreased urine output

**Diagnosis:** diagnostic ascitic tap

- > 250 neutrophils/µL or TLC > 1000/µL
- Ascitic fluid culture positive

> \*R/o secondary bacterial peritonitis due to perforation if patient has severe symptoms.

**Management of SBP**

- Inj. Cefotaxime 2 g IV ATD TDS **OR**
- Inj. Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV ATD f/b TDS; 2.25 g TDS (if AKI/CKD)
- Inj. Albumin 20% 1.5 g/kg IV on day 1

#### Massive ascites

- Tense ascites with fluid thrill
- Associated dyspnoea / orthopnoea

**Management**

- Therapeutic paracentesis (up to 5 L) if haemodynamically stable
- Send diagnostic samples to r/o SBP
- Inj. Albumin 20% if > 5 L paracentesis — give 8 g/L tapped
- Expect worsening of HE

#### Hepato-renal syndrome

- Inj. Terlipressin 2 mg q4–12h IV
- Inj. Noradrenaline 0.5–3 mg/kg per hr
- Inj. Albumin 20% 1 g/kg IV
- Treat hypovolemia, infection precipitating AKI

#### Pulmonary oedema / SCAPE

**Fluid overload**

- Insidious onset, gradually progressive symptoms
- Diuretics mainstay of treatment — IV Furosemide 40–60 mg IV

**SCAPE (Sympathetic Crashing Acute Pulmonary Edema)**

- Sudden onset dyspnoea
- Tachycardia, tachypnea, hypertension with bilateral crepitation on chest auscultation
- **Management:** initiate NIV; NTG IV bolus of 400–1000 mcg followed by IV infusion at 100 mcg/min and rapid titration with continuous BP monitoring q10–15 min

#### Accelerated hypertension (BP > 180/110)

Examine for end-organ damage: acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary oedema, stroke, AKI on CKD.

- **If present** — treat with IV medications
- **Else**, oral antihypertensives may be given

Options:

- Tab. Amlodipine 5 mg, 10 mg PO
- Tab. Clonidine (Arkamine) 0.1 mg / 0.2 mg PO
- Tab. Telmisartan 40 mg PO
- Inj. Furosemide (Lasix) 20–80 mg IV
- Inj. Labetalol 10 mg, 20 mg IV
- Inj. Nitroglycerine infusion 25 mg in 25 mL NS @ 0.6 mL/hr (titrated as per BP)

> \*Always consult SR before prescribing any of the above.

#### Indications for emergency dialysis — "AEIOU"

- **A** — Acidosis (severe, refractory)
- **E** — Electrolyte (refractory hyperkalaemia)
- **I** — Ingestions (dialyzable toxins)
- **O** — Overload (fluid overload / pulmonary oedema)
- **U** — Uremic symptoms (encephalopathy etc.)

## 16. Assessment of a patient with Haematological disorder in ED

### Salient history to be taken

- Malaise, generalized weakness, fatigue, dyspnoea on exertion, headache, orthostatic light-headedness, angina, decreased exercise tolerance → **anaemia**
- Fever (recurrent infections) → **leukopenia**
- Petechiae, purpura, mucosal bleeding → **thrombocytopenia**
- Other bleeding manifestation (UGI bleed, haemoptysis, melena etc.)
- Fever, weight loss, night sweats
- **History to localise any focus of infection** (cough, expectoration, pain abdomen, dysuria, vomiting, diarrhoea, headache / seizures / skin lesions, limb redness / swelling, indwelling catheters)
- H/o prior / recent **blood transfusion**
- H/o recent **chemotherapeutic / immunosuppressive medications**
- H/o previous similar illness / family history of illness
- **Co-morbid illness:** tuberculosis, aplastic anaemia, malignancy (haematological / other)

### Salient clinical examination

- Complete primary assessment
- Temperature
- Skin & mucosa: rash, petechiae, ecchymosis, folliculitis / vesicular lesion
- Lymphadenopathy
- Fever or focal sign of infection — lung / urinary tract / per abdomen / joints & soft tissue abscess, cellulitis / indwelling catheter / lines
- Active bleeding, including oral, nasopharyngeal, haemoptysis, vaginal, and gastrointestinal
- Hepatosplenomegaly
- Stigmata of underlying cause of pancytopenia

### Point-of-care investigations

*Investigations to be done based on probable clinical diagnosis.*

- Baseline CBC
- RFT + serum electrolytes
- **Blood culture in all patients of febrile neutropenia**

When indicated:

- Blood gas, lactate
- LFT / PT-INR
- Blood group + cross match
- Chest X-ray (? LRTI)
- Abdomen X-ray and USG if suspecting any intra-abdominal infection **OR** neutropenic enterocolitis
- **POCUS** — focus of infection, pleuro-pericardial effusion, fluid status of patient
- **Peripheral smear** — to evaluate for abnormal cells indicative of leukaemia, lymphoma
- NCCT brain — ? IC bleed
- CECT brain — ? infection, ? CNS metastasis

### 🚩 Red flag signs

- Compromised primary survey
- Shock index > 1 with neutropenia
- Severe anaemia / thrombocytopenia
- High lactate on initial blood gas

### Basic initial management

- IV access (widest bore possible)
- Supplemental oxygen if SpO₂ < 94% or patient in shock
- Shift to monitored area / red area in case of red flags

**Transfusion goals**

- Asymptomatic patients: Hb ≤ 6
- Known CAD: Hb ≤ 8 g/dL
- Symptoms of ACS → Hb 10
- **Thrombocytopenia**
  - Non-bleeding: ≤ 10,000
  - Clinically significant bleeding: ≤ 50,000

> *Do not give IV fluid boluses without SR supervision for a severely anaemic patient.*

### Quick guide to common emergencies in haematological disorders

#### Febrile neutropenia

- A single oral temperature measurement of ≥ 38.3 °C **OR** temperature of ≥ 38.0 °C sustained over a 1-hour period
- **Neutropenia:** ANC < 500 cells/mm³, **OR** an ANC expected to decrease to < 500 cells/mm³ during the next 48 hours
- Look for signs of mucositis, abscesses, skin breakdown, and line infections
- **Take blood cultures & start antibiotics ASAP**

**MASCC score**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Burden of illness: no or mild symptoms | 5 |
| Burden of illness: moderate symptoms | 3 |
| Burden of illness: severe symptoms | 0 |
| No hypotension (systolic BP > 90 mmHg) | 5 |
| No chronic obstructive pulmonary disease | 4 |
| Solid tumour with no previous fungal infection | 4 |
| No dehydration | 3 |
| Age < 60 years | 2 |
| Outpatient status (at onset of fever) | 3 |

- Scores ≥ 21 → low risk of complications → oral antibiotics & close follow-up
- Score < 21 → high-risk patients → broad-spectrum IV antibiotics

Treat source of infection — abscess drainage / removal of indwelling lines which are a probable source.

#### Hyperleucocytosis

- Elevated WBCs > 50,000; typically > 1,00,000/cc
- Typically seen in AML or CML with blast crisis
- **Features of leucostasis:** lung (hypoxia); CNS (vision changes, headache, dizziness, altered sensorium, IC bleed); myocardial ischemia; renal failure; DIC
- **Classic triad:** mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, vaginal/rectal bleeding, haematuria), visual disturbances, and altered consciousness

**Management**

- Maintain hydration
- Prophylaxis with allopurinol (Tab 100 mg BD)
- Treat hyperkalaemia
- Tab. Hydroxyurea 50–100 mg/kg/day **OR** induction chemotherapy (as per SR Haematology advice)

**Transfusion goals:** Hb 5–6 g/dL; platelet 10,000. *Excessive transfusion precipitates hyperviscosity symptoms.*

#### Tumour lysis syndrome

- H/o recent chemotherapy
- Anorexia, lethargy, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, haematuria, seizures
- Hyperuricemia (> 7)
- Hyperkalaemia (> 6)
- Hyperphosphatemia (> 4.5)
- Hypocalcaemia (< 8)
- Acute kidney injury
- Cardiac arrhythmia / sudden death
- Seizures

**Management**

- High-volume IV crystalloid to maintain urine output & prevent AKI
- Tab. Allopurinol 100 mg BD (up to 800 mg/day)
- Anti-hyperkalaemia measures
- Rasburicase (Inj. 6 mg over 30 mins; single dose)
- Treat symptomatic hypocalcaemia
- Renal replacement (haemodialysis) if not responding to the above

## 17. Medications for treating Hyperkalaemia (S. K⁺ > 5.5 mEq/L)

8. \***Inj. FUROSEMIDE** 40 to 80 mg IV stat (avoid in anuric patients and in hypotension)
9. If the patient is in cardiac arrest, all the above drugs (steps 3 to 7) will be given as IV bolus

## 18. Initial loading dose for suspected ACS patient

> **Expedite ECG within 10 minutes of patient's arrival. Inform SR Emergency Medicine and show the ECG.**

3. **Tab CLOPIDOGREL** 300 mg (4 tablets of 75 mg) PO stat
4. **Tab ATORVASTATIN** 80 mg (2 tablets of 40 mg) PO stat

- **Avoid aspirin + clopidogrel:** massive upper GI bleed, suspected stroke, known aspirin allergy
- Patients unable to take orally: all the above medications can be given through NG tube
- In patients being taken up for **primary PCI**, Tab Clopidogrel **600 mg** PO dose is given

7. **Nasogastric tube lavage** (14, 16 or 18") with 10 mL/kg NS, to see for active bleed (bright red colour) — \*to be done only when airway is stable and haemodynamic resuscitation is initiated

### Diarrhoea

1. **Non-bloody:** no antibiotics recommended
   > **DO NOT prescribe ciprofloxacin or metronidazole for every case of diarrhoea.**
2. **Bloody diarrhoea or fever:**
   - Orally tolerating: Tab CIPROFLOXACIN 500 mg PO BD **OR** Tab CEFIXIME 200 mg PO BD for 3 to 5 days
   - Not tolerating orally: Inj. CIPROFLOXACIN 200 mg IV BD ± Inj. METRONIDAZOLE 500 mg IV TDS

### Liver abscess

1. **Inpatient management** (left lobe, size > 5 cm, percutaneous drainage possible, ruptured, subcapsular location, haemodynamically unstable):
   - Inj. CEFTRIAXONE 1 g IV ATD BD
   - Inj. METRONIDAZOLE 500 mg IV TDS
2. **Outpatient management** (no indication of inpatient admission):
   - Tab CEFIXIME 200 mg PO BD **AND** Tab METRONIDAZOLE 800 mg PO TDS × 7–10 days

### Suspected peritonitis

1. **Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis** (as in CLD: frank pus, > 250 neutrophils/µL or TLC > 1000/µL in ascitic tap):
   - Inj. CEFOTAXIME 2 g IV ATD TDS
2. **Secondary bacterial peritonitis** (e.g. perforation, post-op etc.):
   - Inj. PIPERACILLIN-TAZOBACTAM (PIPTAZ) 4.5 g IV ATD TDS (in AKI/CKD give 2.25 g IV TDS)

### Cellulitis

1. **Inpatient management** (haemodynamic instability, systemic toxicity, rapid progression):
   - Inj. PIPTAZ 4.5 g IV ATD TDS (in AKI/CKD, give 2.25 g IV TDS)
   - Inj. VANCOMYCIN (15 mg/kg) 1 g IV in 100 mL NS over 1 hour BD **OR** Inj. CLINDAMYCIN 900 mg IV TDS (if Staph / Streptococcal infection is suspected)
2. **Outpatient management** (no indication of admission, tolerating orally):
   - Tab AMOXICLAV 625 mg PO TDS × 5 days

### Meningitis

1. Inj. CEFTRIAXONE 2 g IV ATD
2. Inj. VANCOMYCIN 2 g IV in 100 mL NS over 1 hour BD
3. Inj. AMPICILLIN 2 g IV q4h (add if old age, malignancy, pregnant)
4. Inj. DEXAMETHASONE 10 mg IV QID (1st dose just before / with 1st dose of antibiotics)
5. Inj. ACYCLOVIR (10 mg/kg) 600 mg IV TDS (in suspected viral encephalitis, commonly HSV)

### Malaria

1. **Severe** (haemodynamic instability, ANY organ involvement):
   - Inj. ARTESUNATE 2.4 mg/kg (120 mg) IV at 0 / 12 / 24 hr, then q24h
2. **Non-severe *P. vivax*:**
   - Tab CHLOROQUINE 300 mg base PO: 2 tabs on Day 1 & 2, and 1 tab on Day 3
   - Tab PRIMAQUINE (0.25 mg/kg) 15 mg PO OD for 14 days (r/o G6PD deficiency before PQ initiation)
3. **Non-severe *P. falciparum*:**
   - Oral ACT (e.g. ARTEMETHER 80 mg and LUMEFANTRINE 480 mg) — one FDC tablet BD for 3 days **AND**
   - Tab PRIMAQUINE (0.75 mg/kg) 45 mg (3 tablets of 15 mg) PO on Day 2

### Enteric fever

1. **Inpatient management** (not tolerating orally, organ involvement):
   - Inj. CEFTRIAXONE 1 g IV ATD BD
2. **Outpatient management:**
   - Tab CEFIXIME 400 mg (2 tabs of 200 mg) PO BD for 10–14 days **AND**
   - Tab AZITHROMYCIN 1 g (2 tabs of 500 mg) for 5 days

### Suspected sepsis, fever with multi-organ involvement

> **Ask SR in charge for appropriate antibiotics.**

Common empirical regimens:

- **(a)** Inj. PIPTAZ 4.5 g IV ATD TDS **OR** Inj. MEROPENEM 1 g IV ATD TDS **OR** Inj. CEFOPERAZONE-SULBACTAM (Magnex) 2 g IV ATD TDS
- **plus (b)** Inj. VANCOMYCIN 15 mg/kg (1 g) IV in 100 mL NS over 1 hr **OR** Inj. TEICOPLANIN (Targocid) 400 mg IV in 100 mL NS over 1 hr at 0 / 12 / 24 hour and then OD
- **(c)** Inj. DOXYCYCLINE 100 mg IV BD (same dose orally acceptable)

### Febrile neutropenia (fever plus neutropenia < 1000/mcL)

1. **Inpatient (MASCC score < 21):**
   - Inj. CEFOPERAZONE-SULBACTAM (Magnex) 2 g IV ATD TDS **and**
   - Inj. AMIKACIN (15 mg/kg) 750 mg IV OD
2. **Outpatient (MASCC score > 21):**
   - Tab AMOXICILLIN-CLAVULANIC ACID (625 mg) PO TDS **AND**
   - Tab LEVOFLOXACIN 750 mg PO OD for 3 days → OPD follow-up

### Urinary tract infection (UTI)

1. **Uncomplicated UTI** (no structural or functional abnormality in urinary tract):
   - Tab NITROFURANTOIN 100 mg PO BD **OR** Tab CEFIXIME 400 mg PO BD **OR** Tab CIPROFLOXACIN 500 mg PO BD for 5 days
2. **Pyelonephritis, catheter-related, or patient is sick:**
   - Inj. PIPTAZ (Piperacillin-Tazobactam) 4.5 g IV ATD TDS
   - Inj. MEROPENEM 1 g IV ATD TDS

### Renal modification of specific antibiotics (ask SR)

- Inj. VANCOMYCIN
- Inj. PIPERACILLIN-TAZOBACTAM
- Inj. AMIKACIN
- Inj. MEROPENEM
